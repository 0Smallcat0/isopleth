    How it works
    
  
    A nomogram is a calculator printed on paper. Each variable of the formula is a scale; the scales are drawn so that any three values that satisfy the formula lie on one straight line.
    
  
    Maurice d'Ocagne named and systematised the method in 1884. Engineering handbooks, flight manuals and clinical references printed nomograms until pocket calculators replaced them in the 1970s.
    
  
    To read one, lay a straightedge across two known values and read the answer where it crosses the third scale. Here you can drag any handle; the one you touched least recently is solved.
    
  
    Every sheet states its own accuracy: Isopleth moves the straightedge by the stated error at 1,681 points of the chart and reports the worst reading, with a 10 % margin.
    
  
    The printed chart needs no power, no device and no network. Check the 100 mm bar on the print before use.
